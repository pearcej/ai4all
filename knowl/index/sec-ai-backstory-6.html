In 1950, Alan Turing, a British mathematician and computer scientist who is widely considered the father of modern computer science, published his pioneering paper Computing Machinery and Intelligence, introducing ideas that would later become associated with the Turing Test. A few years later, John McCarthy, an American computer scientist, coined the term artificial intelligence and later described it as “the science and engineering of making intelligent machines.” These developments culminated in the Dartmouth Conference in 1956, which brought together researchers from multiple disciplines to explore the possibility of thinking machines. The conference is widely regarded as the birth of AI as a distinct field of study.
